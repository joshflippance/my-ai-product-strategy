The Case · 1 of 3

Why now

Cottage Book is already live at cottagebook.ca with the pieces a fair draft needs: bookings, family branches with shares, and a shared cost ledger, all under tenant isolation.
In the last year models became good enough to read a free-text wish list ("nothing before July 23", "the wedding weekend") and explain a tradeoff in plain words, at about 0.60 CAD a season. Family Cottage, the closest competitor, sells branches, fairness and expense splits to co-owners but has no AI draft yet. Why this team: a 20-year SaaS product leader who built and runs the product solo, already inside the Eels Lake cottage community where the first outside owners come from. The window is the spring 2027 planning season; miss it and the bet waits a year.
The Case · 2 of 3

What's defensible

Honestly, not much yet.
A free chatbot can draft a rough season today, and the flywheel scores 6/20 against Family Cottage's 7/20. The defense is the record, not the model (M2): drafts that become real bookings, a code guardrail that checks every week against each family's hard limits, and corrections plus a multi-season fairness history that make next year's draft better and leaving costly. None of that compounds until correction capture ships, so it ships inside the beta, not after it.
The Case · 3 of 3

The economics

About 0.60 CAD of AI per settled season against a 20 CAD outcome price, about 97% gross on the add-on, and above 90% even at 3x inference prices (M3).
Card fees on a 20 CAD charge (about 0.90 CAD at an assumed 2.9% plus 0.30) cost more than the AI. The revenue is small: about 485 CAD a year at an assumed 100 cottages, with no sized market or path to 100 yet. So the case is retention, not revenue: does a co-owned cottage with a settled season and a fairness record stay? Token costs, the 25% co-owned share and willingness to pay are all still assumptions the beta replaces with measurements.

The Risks

What could break this

Trust / Failure
The headline failure is a draft that gives a family a week they said they couldn't do, or tells every member about another branch's private plans in a cottage set to hide bookings. The M4 contract catches both: a code guardrail on every hard limit, per-week confidence (Clear, Close call, Needs your call), no lock-in until the organiser resolves contested weeks, and a 27-row golden set with 7 adversarial cases that blocks release on any breach. Weakness the evaluator flagged: every golden row is synthetic and CI isn't running yet, so beta wish lists become golden rows.
Scale / Governance
At 10x, AI cost stays in cents; what breaks is one founder as on-call, reviewer and privacy officer, and Quebec Law 25 the moment a Quebec resident joins (M5). Governance is written down: AI drafts on its own, but locking in, sending and charging need a human, and the one agent can't merge. All three learning loops are inactive today, and the shadow AI audit found zero confirmed workarounds, so the signals are hypotheses until families use it.
Competitive
Family Cottage adds an AI draft on the co-owner data it already holds within 6 to 12 months, before Cottage Book has a fairness record worth staying for. The kill: fewer than 3 of 5 beta cottages settle a season with 2 or fewer week changes, or 5 named cottages can't be recruited by 31 January 2027.
The Roadmap

How this moves through time

Horizon 1

  • Recruit 5 named co-owned cottages from the Eels Lake launch and offer codes, and interview each about how they settled last summer
  • Season Draft beta with correction capture in the spring 2027 planning window: every draft saved next to the final schedule, "Your call" edits fed into redrafts (M1 bet, M2 weakest loop)

Horizon 2

Horizon 3
Bet · 9-18 months
  • Multi-season fairness ledger: who got which holiday and nights against share carry across years, so leaving means losing the record (M2 defense)
  • Network intelligence: anonymous cross-cottage patterns (how holiday clashes settle) set defaults for new cottages, through an aggregate-only function that keeps tenant isolation (M2, M5)
